EWMA workload ratio

Log each session, and the tool compares your recent training load (a 7-day weighted average) with your longer-term load (a 28-day weighted average) to show how quickly your workload is changing.

Log a session

Session load = minutes × RPE. Rate it 20–30 minutes after training. Days with no entry count as rest days (zero load). Two sessions on one day add up. Sessions are saved in this browser only.

What the zones mean

Below 0.80Low load. Your recent workload is below your usual average. Consider your training goals and recovery.

0.80–1.30Moderate. Your recent workload is close to your longer-term average. Keep monitoring fatigue and recovery.

1.30–1.50Elevated. Your recent workload is higher than usual. Watch fatigue and recovery before increasing load.

Above 1.50Very high. Your recent workload is much higher than usual. Review your training and recovery with your coach or a sports professional.

These are commonly used illustrative ranges, not medical thresholds. Injury risk depends on many factors, and ACWR alone cannot determine it.

Method

Load. Each session’s load is duration in minutes × session RPE (the session rating of perceived exertion, 1–10). Daily load is the sum of that day’s sessions, and days without sessions are zero.

Weighted average (EWMA). Each day’s load is blended with the previous average: EWMAt = λ·loadt + (1 − λ)·EWMAt−1, with λ = 2 ÷ (N + 1), using N = 7 for acute and N = 28 for chronic. Recent days count more than older ones. The ratio is acute ÷ chronic.

Limits. This uses self-reported data and a simple model. ACWR is debated in the research literature, so treat it as one monitoring signal alongside how you feel, sleep, and what your coach sees.

About

Hi, I’m Sebastian Sotelo, a soccer player from Panama.

I had the idea to make a load management and injury prevention tool for people who don’t have the best resources. Sports scientists, GPS vests, and wearables are expensive, and many athletes train hard without any of them. I wanted to show that a simple, free tool can still help someone keep an eye on their training.

What makes it accessible. All you need is a phone or computer. You log how long you trained and how hard it felt (RPE), and the tool does the math. There are no sensors to buy, no account to create, and nothing to pay for.

Why EWMA. I use a weighted average so that recent training counts more than older training, which makes the ratio more responsive to sudden changes in workload.

Your privacy. Your sessions are stored only in your own browser. Nothing is sent to a server.

Where this is going. This is a project I’m still building and improving. It’s meant to help athletes and coaches monitor training load, and it is not medical advice. If you have pain or an injury, talk to a qualified professional.
